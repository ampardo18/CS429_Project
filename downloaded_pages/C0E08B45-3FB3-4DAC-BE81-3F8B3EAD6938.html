The Karantina massacre (Arabic: مجزرة الكرنتينا; French: Massacre de La Quarantaine/Karantina) took place on January 18, 1976, early in the Lebanese Civil War.[6] La Quarantine, known in Arabic as Karantina, was a Muslim-inhabited district in mostly Christian East Beirut controlled by forces of the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO),[7] and inhabited by Palestinians, Kurds, Armenians, Syrians, and Lebanese Sunnis.[8][9] The fighting and subsequent killings also involved an old Quarantine area near the port and nearby Maslakh quarter.[10][5][11]

Karantina was overrun by militias of the right-wing and mostly Christian Lebanese Front, specifically the Kataeb Regulatory Forces (KRF) militia of the Kataeb Party (a.k.a. Phalangists),[12][13] resulting in the deaths of approximately 600–1,500 people.[2][5] According to then-Washington Post-correspondent Jonathan Randal, "Many Lebanese Muslim men and boys were rounded up and separated from the women and children and massacred, while the women and young girls were violently raped and robbed."[11]

The Damour massacre two days later was a reprisal for the Karantina massacre.[5][14]

After Kataeb Regulatory Forces (KRF), Guardians of the Cedars (GoC), National Liberal Party's Tigers Militia and Lebanese Youth Movement (LYM) forces took control of the Karantina district, the Tel al-Zaatar refugee camp was besieged for five months, ending in the Tel al-Zaatar massacre.[4]
